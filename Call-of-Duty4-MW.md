# Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare on Low-End Hardware

Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare is a game worth noting. I played it on the very
same laptop where I ran Left 4 Dead 1 and 2, an Acer ES1-523, which made it a
fair comparison. The difference was clear: CoD4's graphics looked much better,
and the gameplay felt far smoother than Left 4 Dead 2 on the Source engine. 
Both games ran on my machine without much crashing, but CoD4 was really something else.
Part of the reason is the engine. CoD4 was built by Infinity Ward on IW 3.0, a 
heavily modified descendant of the Quake 3 engine, and it was designed around
a steady 60 frames per second in tight, controlled environments. Left 4 Dead 2
asks much more of the hardware at once, with large hordes, dynamic lighting,
and physics running together. A weak laptop feels that difference immediately.
CoD4 controls what appears on screen, so it stays smooth even when the hardware struggles.

Even so, my favorite is still Left 4 Dead when it comes to storyline and replayability. 
CoD4 is a polished, scripted experience, but Left 4 Dead creates its moments through 
the Director, so every run feels different. For me, CoD4 wins on graphics and smoothness, 
while Left 4 Dead wins on the experience of playing it. As an aspiring game developer, 
I want to study both: how CoD4 stays so smooth, and how Left 4 Dead keeps its pacing alive.
